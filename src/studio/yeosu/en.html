Overview

An export company whose website made it hard for buyers to get in touch.

Yeosu Saegomak processes cockles from Yeoja Bay in Yeosu and exports them overseas. On its old website, the path from seeing a product to sending an inquiry was hard to find. I defined that problem and proposed it before our first meeting, then took the work through information architecture and a design system to page design and frontend build.

Problem Definition

I started by defining the problem

I audited 34 screens of the old site, grouped what I found into four problems, and paired each with a direction. What follows is that proposal, rebuilt for this page.

The old site

0101

Visual and technical decay

  • No HTTPS, so browsers showed security warnings
  • Built on an eight-year-old web framework (Bootstrap 4)
  • Pinch-zoom blocked on mobile (an accessibility violation)
  • Unoptimized JPGs everywhere, slowing every page
0202

Misleading information architecture

  • Four "Business" cards on the home page all led to the same page
  • Desktop, mobile and footer menus were defined separately
  • Bulletin-board structure leaked into URLs, confusing users and maintainers
0303

No path to conversion

  • No purchase or inquiry button on the home page
  • No search and no category filters
  • Products, recipes and social were disconnected, so B2B and B2C leads went nowhere
0404

No brand consistency

  • A generic Gnuboard template with no brand identity
  • No brand color or type system
  • Clean waters and fishermen shareholders, the brand’s real assets, never came across
UX Solution

Cut, reduce,
rebuild.

01

User-first design

Drop the supplier-centered menu and rebuild navigation around the tasks visitors look for first.

02

Mobile first

Mobile is 70% of traffic, so design for it first and deliver a fully responsive view.

03

Data-driven IA

Base the structure on GA4 data and heatmaps, not hunches.

04

A design system the team can run

Go beyond one-off pages and hand over a component library the in-house team can combine and extend.

Target

Who comes in, what they check, where they go

Visitors fell into three groups: overseas buyers, domestic partners and consumers. Each wanted to check something different and go somewhere different. The goal was to get every one of them through the information they need and straight to their next step.

Design Concept

Solid, expert,
and global.

I set three impressions as the design criteria, so an overseas buyer seeing the company for the first time could judge whether it was worth doing business with.

Solid.

Deep navy, a blue gradient and heavy headlines for a trustworthy first impression.

Expert.

The HACCP facility and 13-step production process, shown in real photos.

Global.

An export catalog, markets and certifications that answer a buyer’s questions first.

Information Architecture

Rebuilt around brands, products and inquiries

What I learned

When the client asked for a site as dynamic as a big corporation's, what they really wanted was less the motion itself than to look like a company buyers could trust. I learned to confirm the goal behind a request before acting on its wording.

How I Worked

AI was the design tool. I set the direction and the standards

Instead of Figma mockups, I designed directly in code with Codex and Claude Code. I set the design rules first and kept the AI inside them.

01 · AI Agents

From research to QA, split by role and run at once

I ran separate AI agents for research, planning, design, frontend and QA in parallel, so work that would take a long time in sequence moved forward together.

02 · DESIGN.md

I wrote documents before code

Before building, I wrote specs such as DESIGN SITEMAP COPY TONE MOTION, plus an audit of the old site’s structure. Every later change was measured against them.

03 · Human in the Loop

Anything outside the spec, the AI had to stop and ask

So the AI wouldn’t fill gaps on its own, it stopped and asked whenever a decision was needed. We talked briefly, I set the direction, and then it built.

04 · Variations

AI made the options. I made the call

Even for a single production banner, I had four versions built (floating cards, diagonal split, timeline and accordion), put them side by side and picked one.
