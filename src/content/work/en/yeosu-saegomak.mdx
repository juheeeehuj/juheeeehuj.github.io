<Block kicker="Background" title="A corporate site designed straight in code, with no mockup file" sub="What happens when a designer skips the Figma handoff and drives AI coding tools all the way to a finished site?" layout="side" big>
Yeosu Saegomak is a fishermen-owned company that processes cockles from Yeoja Bay and supplies food companies and restaurants in Korea and overseas. The old site had no HTTPS, exposed raw bulletin-board URLs, and offered no path to an inquiry, which made it hard to earn trust from overseas buyers. For the renewal I skipped the Figma mockup stage. I gave Codex and Claude Code written planning and design specs and had them build the screens directly in code, while I set the direction and corrected the results.

<Block kicker="How I worked" title="Twelve spec documents first, decisions in conversation, variations in code" layout="side">
Documents came before code. I analysed the old site's information architecture and wrote twelve spec files, including DESIGN, SITEMAP, COPY, TONE and MOTION, before any implementation started. I told the AI to stop and ask whenever a decision was not covered by the specs, settled each one in a short conversation, and then handed over the build. When a result did not work, I rolled back to the previous version and asked for a different variation. For the production-process banner alone I had four versions built, floating cards, a diagonal split, a timeline and an accordion, compared them side by side and kept one. A design-system document written midway became the reference for every later change.

<Block id="sub" toc="Subpages" kicker="Subpages" title="Twenty-eight subpages, from company overview to contact">
Company overview, history, production process and quality, three brands, products, export information, news and IR, and contact: 28 subpages built on the same rules. Page intros, section labels, cards and the badge system are unified, and press articles from the old bulletin board were migrated only where the original external links were still alive.

<Block kicker="Motion and video" title="I took apart the motion on four reference sites and chose GSAP" layout="side">
I inspected the actual scripts on four reference sites, CJ, Hoban Cultural Foundation, Samsung Welstory and Haatz, to see which libraries drove which motion, and picked GSAP ScrollTrigger, which three of the four use. The hero text reveal, product-card parallax, scroll-linked movement in the brand section and the number counters all came from that audit. The hero video was generated with Runway and Gemini and placed as the background.
