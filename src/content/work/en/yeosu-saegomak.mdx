<Block kicker="Background" title="Redesigning a website that was driving visitors away" sub="Four diagnoses: information architecture, ageing technology, no conversion path, no brand consistency. And this time, designed in code instead of Figma." layout="side" big>
Yeosu Saegomak is a fishermen-owned company that processes cockles from Yeoja Bay and supplies food companies and restaurants in Korea and overseas. An audit of the existing site surfaced four problems. Information architecture: four business-area cards on the home page all led to the same page, and the bulletin-board structure leaked into the URLs. Ageing technology: no HTTPS, an eight-year-old Bootstrap 4 base, and pinch-zoom blocked on mobile. Conversion: no purchase or inquiry button anywhere, so B2B and B2C leads had nowhere to go. Brand consistency: a generic bulletin-board template with no brand identity, so assets like clean waters and fishermen shareholders never came across.

The redesign took four directions: user-first design, mobile first, data-driven information architecture and a design system the client can operate. And I skipped the Figma mockup stage. I gave Codex and Claude Code written planning and design specs and had them build the screens directly in code, while I set the direction and corrected the results.

<Block kicker="How I worked" title="Twelve spec documents first, decisions in conversation, variations in code" layout="side">
The proposal offered two directions, a refined, trustworthy and balanced A and a solid, professional and global B, and recommended A as the base with B's big typography and motion borrowed in. In the build, documents came before code. I analysed the old site's information architecture and wrote twelve spec files, including DESIGN, SITEMAP, COPY, TONE and MOTION, before any implementation started. I told the AI to stop and ask whenever a decision was not covered by the specs, settled each one in a short conversation, and then handed over the build. When a result did not work, I rolled back to the previous version and asked for a different variation. For the production-process banner alone I had four versions built, floating cards, a diagonal split, a timeline and an accordion, compared them side by side and kept one. A design-system document written midway became the reference for every later change.

<Block id="sub" toc="Subpages" kicker="Subpages" title="Twenty-eight subpages, from company overview to contact">
Company overview, history, production process and quality, three brands, products, export information, news and IR, and contact: 28 subpages built on the same rules. Page intros, section labels, cards and the badge system are unified, and press articles from the old bulletin board were migrated only where the original external links were still alive.

<Block kicker="Motion and video" title="I took apart the motion on four reference sites and chose GSAP" layout="side">
I inspected the actual scripts on four reference sites, CJ, Hoban Cultural Foundation, Samsung Welstory and Haatz, to see which libraries drove which motion, and picked GSAP ScrollTrigger, which three of the four use. The hero text reveal, product-card parallax, scroll-linked movement in the brand section and the number counters all came from that audit. The hero video was generated with Runway and Gemini and placed as the background.
