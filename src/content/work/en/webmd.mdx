<Block kicker="Background" title="Why Is Searching for Health Information So Stressful?" sub="Can a Mild Symptom Like a Headache Trigger Unnecessary Anxiety?" layout="side" big>
When people feel unwell, they often search online before seeing a doctor, and tools like WebMD can increase anxiety when information feels unclear or overwhelming. This project focuses on improving information structure and interaction flow to help users feel more confident when making health decisions.

<Feature before="webmd/main-feature-before-symptom.png" after="webmd/main-feature-symptom" beforeLabel="As is" afterLabel="To be" caption="Symptom Input" title="More Specific Symptom Questions">

<Feature before="webmd/main-feature-before-results.png" after="webmd/main-feature-results" beforeLabel="As is" afterLabel="To be" caption="Possible Conditions" title="Data-Informed Results">

<Phase id="discover" label="Discover">WebMD’s Symptom Checker Provides a Lot of Health Information. But Are Users Able to Use It Effectively?</Phase>

<Block kicker="Secondary Research" title="Why WebMD Fails to Support Confident Decisions: Too Much Information, Too Few Symptom Questions">
We reviewed 23 academic papers on digital health decision-making behavior related to WebMD to understand why users struggle to make decisions with WebMD. We assumed that too much information with too few questions leads to lower accuracy and more difficult decision-making.

<Block kicker="App Review Analysis" title="Recurring Confusion and Low Trust in Results" layout="side">
We conducted affinity mapping on user reviews and identified recurring frustration with result accuracy, as well as difficulty determining what actions to take based on the information provided.

<Block id="final-designs" toc="Final Designs" kicker="Final Designs" title="The Solution: Trust and Clarity in Health Decisions" big />

<Feature after="webmd/final-design-1" caption="Symptom Input" kicker="Final Design 1/4" title="Progressive Questions">
Guides users through step-by-step, context-aware questions. Users can enter details such as symptom duration, pain type, and pain level, and select additional symptoms they might otherwise miss.
</Feature>

<Feature after="webmd/final-design-2" caption="Possible Conditions" kicker="Final Design 2/4" title="Prioritized Results">
Visualizes results based on user responses to help users quickly understand possible conditions. Users can filter conditions by severity and sort results by top matches, frequency, or rarity.
</Feature>

<Feature after="webmd/final-design-3" caption="Condition Details" kicker="Final Design 3/4" title="Scannable Medical Content">
Organizes medical information into clear, accordion sections for easier scanning. Users can choose which sections to view and track symptoms directly from the condition detail page.
</Feature>

<Feature after="webmd/final-design-4" caption="Visual Reference" kicker="Final Design 4/4" title="Visual Learning Support">
Provides visual references to improve understanding of medical terms, while allowing users to control whether they view sensitive content.
</Feature>

<Block id="usability-test" toc="Usability Test" kicker="Usability Test" title="Evaluating Clarity and Confidence in Use">
We conducted a usability test with 8 participants across 5 key tasks to observe how users interacted with the redesigned experience in real scenarios. The product achieved an average SUS score of 84.38.

<div slot="media">
<Row items={[{ src: 'webmd/usability-test', caption: 'Usability Testing' }, { src: 'webmd/usability-session.png', caption: 'Usability Test with Participant A' }]} />

High-scoring participants felt the redesigned flow provided enough clarity to trust the results and decide what to do next. However, lower scores often came from moments when guidance was hard to notice. This suggests that clearer visual cues could reduce hesitation and help users feel more confident.

<Figure src="webmd/sus-score.png" caption="SUS Score" />
</div>
</Block>

<Block kicker="Iteration" title="Iterating for Better Clarity and Confidence">
Based on usability testing, I identified moments where users hesitated due to unclear guidance and information priority. To address this, I refined the interface by making key messages more visible and reorganizing content to better match user expectations.
</Block>

<Feature before="webmd/iteration-initial-1.png" after="webmd/iteration-refined-1.png" beforeLabel="Initial Version" afterLabel="Refined Version" caption="Inline Validation Feedback" kicker="Symptom Selection Guidance" quote="I didn’t notice the message that says I need to select at least two symptoms." arrow>
Users often missed the minimum symptom requirement, which caused hesitation. To improve visibility, I added visual emphasis using color and borders, making the guidance easier to notice at the right moment.
</Feature>

<Feature before="webmd/iteration-initial-2.png" after="webmd/iteration-refined-2.png" beforeLabel="Initial Version" afterLabel="Refined Version" caption="Condition Details" kicker="Medical Information Prioritization" quote="I’m more curious about how to treat this than seeing an image." arrow>
Users were primarily looking for actionable information. Based on this feedback, I moved treatment-related content higher in the layout so users could quickly find what they cared about most.
</Feature>

<Takeaways kicker="Takeaways" title="What I Focused On">
<Takeaway n="01" title="Information Structure Shapes Health Decisions">
I realized that how medical information is structured directly affects users’ understanding, trust, and ability to act. Clear hierarchy, progressive disclosure, and scannable content are not visual choices, but critical design decisions that shape real-world health behavior.
</Takeaway>

<Takeaway n="02" title="Designing for Trust, Not Just Accuracy">
In healthcare, perceived trust is as important as technical accuracy. Through this project, I learned to design experiences that help users feel confident in the process — by making reasoning visible, prioritization clear, and outcomes understandable.
</Takeaway>

<Takeaway n="03" title="Privacy as a Core UX Principle">
I realized that in healthcare, privacy is not a feature but a foundation. By designing optional exposure to sensitive content and giving users control over visibility, I learned how UX can respect dignity while still supporting understanding.
</Takeaway>
</Takeaways>
